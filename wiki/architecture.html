02 · Architecture

How the system fits together

Venus OS models devices on its local D-Bus. Bridges and companion applications use MQTT, HTTP or WebSockets according to their own integration contracts. The repository map is not a live deployment inventory.

The five layers

1 · Hardware & sensing

ESP32s, Tasmota plugs, CT clamps, Emporia Vue submeters, EV chargers and water tanks/pumps provide readings through firmware or configured Home Assistant entities. esphome-jbd-bms-mqttdbus-esphome-grid-sensordbus-evchargerdbus-pump

2 · D-Bus services

Translators running on the GX: MQTT→D-Bus for the audited battery and Tasmota PV bridges, and Home Assistant→D-Bus for Emporia, EV, charger and pump measurements. Third-party hardware appears as Victron device services. dbus-mqtt-batterydbus-tasmota-pvdbus-emporia-vue

3 · Control & policy

inverter-control calculates setpoints and applies its configured validity, limit and watchdog policies before D-Bus writes. It consumes EV data published by the Home Assistant bridges. The archived venus-os-governance project does not gate this deployed path, and no dbus-event-log service was running on the audited GX. These repositories must not be treated as an installed authorization or audit layer. inverter-controlvenus-os-governancedbus-event-logdbus-ev

4 · MQTT distribution

State leaves the GX over MQTT topics. fastapi-mqtt-gateway adds REST/WebSocket access for clients that can't speak MQTT natively. fastapi-mqtt-gateway

5 · Consumption

Dashboards (Go binary on-GX, Python/Docker, Vue SPA, Tauri desktop), Home Assistant, observability stacks and optional forecasting tools. Verify each configured caller before assuming forecasts affect controller decisions. inverter-dashboard-gosolar-forecast-langgraphvenus-os-observabilityinverter-monitoring

Why two buses instead of one

D-Bus is how Venus OS itself models the world — battery, PV inverter, grid meter all appear as com.victronenergy.* services, so the built-in ESS logic treats third-party hardware as first-class. That's non-negotiable for control.

But D-Bus is local-only. Dashboards on a laptop, Home Assistant, Grafana, and forecasters live elsewhere — they subscribe to MQTT mirrors of the same state. The split keeps safety-critical paths on the bus the OS controls, and convenience paths on the protocol every tool already has a client for.

Control path vs telemetry path

Control path (amber)Telemetry path (cyan)
ProtocolD-Bus on-deviceMQTT / REST / WebSocket
WritersConfigured controller and native Venus servicesPer-service publishers and gateways
AuditVerify actual logging; event-log is an optional integrationConfigured metrics and logs
TimingControl cadence plus D-Bus latencySource-specific freshness limits
Failure modeConfigured validity, hold and watchdog policyMark unavailable inputs; stale telemetry can affect control

Cross-cutting concerns

  • Forecasting: optional forecast consumers need a configured, tested control contract; repository availability alone does not close a control loop.
  • AI access: mcp-venus-os provides its own validation and write-confirmation rules. Verify its deployment and transport separately from the controller.
  • EV data path: dbus-ev and dbus-evcharger read Home Assistant and publish D-Bus values for inverter-control. HA outages still require unavailable-value and recovery handling.
  • Testing: broker and simulated D-Bus integration tests complement installer checks and device observation. Firmware compatibility and cold-boot recovery need target-device validation.
